## Design Context

### Users
Three main audiences, all arriving with some context already:
- **Camp/nonprofit directors evaluating a consultant** — time-pressed decision-makers scanning for credibility (30+ years of camp experience, social work background) before committing budget to staff training.
- **HR/culture leads at youth orgs** — staff tasked with actually improving culture day-to-day, looking for practical, usable tools rather than abstract theory.
- **Post-presentation follow-ups** — people who just saw Chris speak or facilitate a training and are returning to learn more or get in touch. Warmest, lowest-skepticism visitors; the site should make it effortless for them to reach out or register.

The Village (a five-circle parent cohort) draws a related but distinct visitor: a parent feeling unsettled by the cultural/political/educational systems around their kids, looking for a steady, reflective space rather than a debate forum.

### Brand Personality
**Warm, grounded, direct.** Rooted in lived experience (social worker, camp director, youth development specialist), not corporate DEI-speak. Confident and plain-spoken — "Love must not be rare" as the closing line of every training says a lot: emotionally direct, not vague or hedged. Compassion paired with real-world practicality, not performative softness.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light throughout — approachable, daytime, browsable during a work day.
- **Tone**: Warm and grounded rather than corporate-consulting-slick or clinical-nonprofit. Should feel like it was made by someone who has actually run a camp for 30 years, not a generic agency.
- **Existing brand colors** (already in the Mantine theme, `app/_theme/colors.ts`): `pc-denim` (blue), `pc-orange`, `pc-shamrock` (green) — a warm, human palette, not corporate navy/gray.
- **Anti-references**: generic nonprofit/consulting site tropes — stock photos of diverse hands stacked together, sterile icon-grid "our values" sections, gradient-heavy SaaS aesthetics. Nothing that reads as templated or interchangeable with any other consultancy.
- No specific reference sites given — favor a distinctive, considered direction over safe defaults.

### Design Principles
1. **Credibility fast, warmth always** — directors and HR leads need to trust Chris's experience within seconds; never sacrifice the warm, direct voice to get there.
2. **Plain-spoken over corporate** — copy and UI language should sound like a person talking, not a consulting deck. Avoid DEI-industry jargon and vague buzzword filler.
3. **Light, human, unpolished-in-the-right-way** — light theme, warm palette, avoid sterile/clinical or generic SaaS patterns (icon grids, gradient text, stock-photo hero clichés).
4. **The Village gets its own register** — same brand family, but calmer and more reflective than the consulting pages, since its visitors arrive anxious rather than evaluating a vendor.
5. **Low-friction contact** — the largest share of traffic (post-presentation follow-ups) is already warm; make reaching out or registering as frictionless as possible, don't bury it.
